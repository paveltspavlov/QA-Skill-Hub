# Stop Slop

## When to Use
Use to revise supplied prose for clarity, naturalness, concision, and specificity. Do not use to conceal uncertainty, alter factual meaning, manufacture a personal voice, or make unsupported content sound authoritative.

## Required Inputs
Source text, intended audience, purpose, desired tone/register, length constraints, terminology to preserve, and whether claims/citations may be changed. If no tone is specified, preserve the source's tone.

## Procedure
1. Read for purpose, claims, evidence, caveats, audience, and author voice before editing.
2. Remove redundant framing, filler, generic conclusions, inflated adjectives, repetitive transitions, empty headings, and needless list items.
3. Replace abstract claims with concrete wording only when the source supports the detail. Prefer active verbs and varied sentence rhythm.
4. Preserve citations, technical terms, uncertainty, quantities, scope, and meaningful repetition. Do not add examples or claims.
5. Return the revision and a concise change note; flag passages that cannot be improved without a factual decision.

## Output Contract
Provide “Revised text” and “Meaning-sensitive edits” with a short list of changes that could affect interpretation. For a short passage, include a before/after sample only if it clarifies a non-obvious edit. If the original is already clear, say so and make minimal or no edits.

## Quality Gate
Every factual claim and caveat in the revision is present in the source or explicitly approved by the user. No qualifier, attribution, citation, or technical distinction is lost. The rewrite is clearer rather than merely shorter.

## Evidence and Uncertainty
Treat the supplied text as the factual boundary. If a sentence is ambiguous, retain its ambiguity and flag it instead of selecting an interpretation. Never create citations, quotes, anecdotes, or author experiences. Apply the evidence-readiness-gate.
